Help

How Gears works. Inspired by Matthias Wandel's gear work at woodgears.ca; for paper templates, his gear template generator at woodgears.ca/gear.

The teeth

  • Module is the tooth size: the pitch diameter is module × teeth, and the outside diameter two modules more. Diametral Pitch is the inch way (teeth per inch of pitch diameter); Circular Pitch is tooth to tooth.
  • The teeth are true involutes, cut as a rack would cut them, so any two gears of the same tooth size and pressure angle mesh. Small gears get the undercut the mating tooth needs.
  • The bottom of each tooth space is rounded to the bit, so a round end mill reaches all of it. That sets the smallest tooth a bit can cut; the Teeth card says what it is, and a bigger bit needs bigger teeth.
  • Backlash leaves play between the teeth; wood and plastic swell and shrink, so give them some. Tip Clearance is room at the root: blank is a quarter module, or more when the rounding the bit leaves at the root needs it, so the other gear’s tips clear it.

Cutting

  • Zero Z on the top of the stock; Total Depth goes a little past its thickness to cut through. Each gear's holes are cut first (cleared from the middle, so nothing comes loose), then its teeth from the outside.
  • Each pass ramps down along the path instead of plunging; Z never comes down faster than the Plunge Rate.
  • A bore is Round, Hex (sized across the flats: 12.7 mm for a 1/2" hex shaft), D (round with one flat, for a D shaft such as a motor's: Across the Flat is from the flat to the far side) or Keyed (a keyway Key Width wide, at least the bit's, Key Depth past the bore). Pick with the menu on its label; type a little over the shaft's size for a slip fit. A round bit can't cut a sharp corner, so each sharp corner gets a small notch for the shaft's or key's corner to sit in.
  • Lightening Holes or Spokes (the menu on the label): round holes in a ring, or that many straight spokes from the hub to the rim with windows between them, their corners rounded a little past the bit. Spoke Width blank picks about an eighth of the rim's radius.
  • Ring Teeth on Gear B's menu makes it a ring with its teeth inside, Gear A its pinion turning inside it, the same way. The ring needs at least 12 more teeth than the pinion at 20° (10 at 25°, 16 at 14.5°). It has a Rim Width outside its teeth, with screw holes (Gear B's Bore and Holes fields); it's cut from inside with tabs on its tooth tips, so the middle stays put, then round its outside.
  • Rack Teeth on Gear B's menu makes it a straight rack, Gear A its pinion: the same teeth, on a bar Rack Height deep, with screw holes (Gear B's Bore and Holes fields) along it, cut from outside with tabs on its bottom edge. The preview rolls the pinion along it; the facts say how far the rack goes a turn and where to mount it from the pinion's axle. Frame Holes is for two gears.
  • Gear B's label menu takes the Ratio A:B instead of its teeth (3 for 3:1): Gear B's teeth are worked out to the nearest whole tooth, with the real ratio shown.
  • Center Distance: blank is the gears' own. Type the distance the axles must be: a little wider spreads the gears to it (more backlash, which the facts show; too wide or too close is refused with the range that works), or press Fit Tooth Size to make the teeth mesh at exactly that distance. Frame Holes drills the axles at it. An idea: Spoilboard Toolbox's center-to-center spacing of your spoilboard's holes or threaded inserts is a distance to fit to, to try a pair on bolts in the spoilboard (for a test only: a bolt's thread wears a bore).
  • Click the Bit Diameter label to fill in the loaded tool's diameter from ncSender's tool table. With a tool changer, the label is a menu of the tool table instead: pick the tool to cut with, and the program starts with a tool change to it (M6 T). Typing another diameter, or No tool change, leaves it out. As in QuickCut.
  • Holding Tabs sit on tooth tips, where they're easy to sand off.
  • Both Gears puts Gear B beside Gear A with the Gap Between them. The Frame Holes tab drills the axles exactly the center distance apart; left blank, a hole is the gear's bore, or for a hex bore the hex's corners, so the shaft turns in it.
  • Programs are in mm; on an inch machine the program ends with G20 to put it back in inches.
